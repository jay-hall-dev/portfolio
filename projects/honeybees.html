How do bees organize themselves?

A swarm makes high-stakes decisions with no individual bee holding the full picture.

InstitutionCU Boulder
Year2026
MethodsComputer Vision, Tracking
ToolsPython · OpenCV · NumPy

The question

A honeybee swarm makes high-stakes decisions — where to build a new nest — with no individual bee holding the full picture. Scout bees survey candidate sites independently and report back through a waggle dance, and somehow the group converges on a single choice. I wanted to understand the mechanics behind that convergence: how local, individual signals turn into a coordinated group outcome.

The experiment

I set up a multi-camera rig to record swarm activity and built a tracking pipeline to extract individual flight trajectories from raw video. The setup needed to handle dense, fast-moving clusters of near-identical insects — which meant a lot of the actual engineering work was in calibration, lighting, and separating real bee motion from camera noise.

The data

Once trajectories were extracted, I looked at flight path density over time to see how consensus emerged spatially — whether agreement built up gradually or shifted suddenly once a threshold of scouts favored one site.

FIG. 03 — Flight-path density over time

The model

I'm working through a simplified agent-based model that treats each bee as following a small set of local rules, to see how much of the swarm's collective behavior can be reproduced without any agent having global information.

What I learned

Individually simple behavior, tracked carefully, gives away a lot about group-level intelligence. A large part of this project turned out to be about tooling — getting clean, high-resolution tracking data was harder than the modeling that came after it.

What's next

Extending the camera rig to stereo tracking for real 3D flight paths, and comparing the agent-based model's output against the recorded consensus timelines.
